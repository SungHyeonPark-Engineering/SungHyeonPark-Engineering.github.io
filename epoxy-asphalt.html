Epoxy asphalt mixture performance

How can mixture strength and resistance to raveling be evaluated?

Research objective

This work evaluated epoxy asphalt mixtures through mixture design and complementary laboratory tests. Strength, raveling resistance, and volumetric characterization were considered as distinct parts of mixture evaluation.

My contribution

  • Designed epoxy asphalt mixtures and prepared test specimens.
  • Performed Marshall stability, indirect tensile strength (ITS), Cantabro loss, and theoretical maximum specific gravity (Gmm) tests.
  • Organized experimental records and contributed to technical reports and manuscript preparation.

Method & research process

  1. 01Mixture design
  2. 02Specimen preparation
  3. 03Four complementary tests

Marshall stability and ITS characterize strength under different loading configurations. Cantabro testing evaluates resistance to material loss, using specimen mass before and after testing. Gmm contributes to volumetric characterization. Together, these tests connected mixture design to strength, material loss, and volumetric properties.

Mixture design & specimen preparation

The preparation workflow moves from weighing to mixing and compaction. These laboratory records illustrate asphalt-mixture methods; the precise material, batch and preparation session associated with each photograph have not all been verified.

Compacted specimen density

This supporting workflow examines volumetric properties for mixture design. Sealing and submerged weighing of a compacted specimen are kept separate from Gmm testing of loose mixture.

Marshall stability testing

This test evaluated the load resistance of cylindrical mixture specimens. I carried out mixture design and specimen preparation and organized the Marshall stability test results in the experimental records.

Indirect tensile strength testing

Diametral loading of a cylindrical specimen was used to evaluate the mixture’s tensile resistance.

Cantabro loss testing

This test evaluated material loss from mixture specimen mass before and after testing. It uses the rotating drum of an LA apparatus for a mixture test, distinct from LA abrasion testing of aggregate. Preparation, conditioning, the drum and weighing are shown first, followed by before-and-after observations from the report.

Theoretical maximum specific gravity · Gmm

Loose mixture was used for theoretical maximum specific gravity measurement and volumetric evaluation. The photographs distinguish weighing from operation of the vacuum apparatus.

Contribution & output

I carried out mixture design and four laboratory tests, organized the experimental records, and contributed to reporting and manuscript preparation. The related study was published in 2024, with me as second and corresponding author.
